How Long Does It Take to Build a Website in British Columbia?

BC Web Creator builds a small business website in 20–30 hours of hands-on work. A site with e-commerce takes 40–60 hours. Those hours cover design, build, content, testing and launch; the calendar window from scope call to launch is set in the written quote, before any work starts.

Hours are not weeks. A 25-hour build is not three working days in a row, because design approval, content and testing happen between the hands-on blocks. What a BC business actually wants to know is when the site goes live, so that window is quoted with the price and held for the length of the project.

The two hour bands

Every project is estimated in hours before it is priced. In practice, BC Web Creator's work falls into two bands, and the band is set by what the site has to do rather than by how it looks.

Small business website: 20–30 hours

A brochure-style site for a business that needs to be found and contacted. Design, a small number of pages, mobile layout, basic SEO setup, testing and launch. Copy supplied by the client, or written as part of the project, is the biggest swing within the band.

E-commerce or booking: 40–60 hours

A site that sells products, takes appointments or gates content. The design and build work is the same shape as a brochure site, then payment, catalogue, availability or login logic sits on top of it, with more testing before launch.

What pushes a project up its band

Extra pages, copywriting from scratch, sourcing photography, third-party integrations (payments, CRM, email), member areas, and additional revision rounds. Each one is scoped before it is quoted, never discovered at the end.

Where the hours actually go

Five blocks of work make up every build. None of them is a separate invoice line or a separate fee; they are the reason a small site is tens of hours rather than a few.

  1. Scope and structure. The scope call, then the page list, navigation and content plan. Getting this wrong is what causes the expensive kind of rework, so it happens before design.
  2. Design. Layout, type, colour and a mobile view, then the revision rounds that get it signed off.
  3. Build. Turning the signed-off design into pages: templates, responsive behaviour, forms, and any e-commerce, booking or integration work.
  4. Content and SEO setup. Placing or writing the text, image preparation, page titles, meta descriptions, structured data and the sitemap.
  5. Testing and launch. Devices and browsers, links, forms and checkout paths where they exist, then DNS cutover, analytics and a final check on the live site.

An e-commerce site does the same five blocks with more in blocks three and five: payment and checkout flows, product and tax settings, order emails, and a test pass for every path a customer can take.

Why the calendar is longer than the hours

Those hours are hands-on work, not consecutive clock time. A build moves between design approval, content, development and testing, and each handoff needs an answer before the next block starts. The launch window quoted with the price accounts for that sequence.

Three things move the date most:

  • Content readiness. Text and photos that exist before the build starts are the single biggest lever; writing and sourcing in parallel with the build stretches the calendar.
  • Feedback speed. Each revision round waits on a decision. Quick answers keep the build moving.
  • Third-party accounts. Domain access, payment processor approval, booking software and email setup all involve someone outside BC Web Creator.

The written quote states the launch window alongside the fixed price, so both are agreed before work starts and neither moves partway through.

Hours are how it is estimated, not how you are billed

Publishing an hour band is not the same as charging by the hour. BC Web Creator estimates a project in hours internally, then converts that estimate into one fixed price in writing. The client is never billed for the hours, never sees a timesheet, and the price does not change if the build runs longer than estimated.

That is the difference between an estimate and an invoice. The hour band sets expectations for how much work a site is; the fixed price is what you agree to pay, and it covers the whole build including the hours it actually takes. Common questions about website build times

How long does it take to build a website in British Columbia?

BC Web Creator builds a small business website in 20–30 hours of hands-on work and an e-commerce site in 40–60 hours. Those hours cover design, build, content, testing and launch. The calendar window from scope call to launch is written into the fixed-price quote before any work starts.

How many hours does a small business website take to build?

A small business website from BC Web Creator takes 20–30 hours of hands-on work: design, build, mobile layout, basic SEO setup, testing and launch on a small number of pages. Projects where the text and photos are ready land at the lower end of that band, because no writing or sourcing has to happen first.

How many hours does an e-commerce website take to build?

An e-commerce website from BC Web Creator takes 40–60 hours of hands-on work. The extra hours sit in the parts a brochure site does not have: product and catalogue setup, payment and checkout flow, shipping and tax settings, and the testing that every one of those paths needs before launch.

Why does an e-commerce site take longer than a brochure site?

An e-commerce site adds payment, checkout, catalogue and order logic on top of the same design and build work a brochure site needs, then multiplies the testing: every product option, payment path and confirmation email has to be tried before launch. That is where the 40–60 hour band comes from.

Do the hours include revisions?

The 20–30 and 40–60 hour bands include the design revision rounds that happen before sign-off, because revisions are part of building the site. Extra rounds, or scope added after the quote is agreed, are quoted as a separate item before the work is done rather than billed by the hour.

Does BC Web Creator charge by the hour?

No. Hours are used to estimate a project internally, then quoted as one fixed price in writing. Clients are never billed for the hours, and the price does not move if the build runs longer than estimated.

Can a website be built faster if I supply the content?

Yes. Supplying your own text and photos removes the writing and image-sourcing work that stretches the calendar most, so the launch window moves in. The hour band stays the same, because design, build, testing and launch still have to happen on the same number of pages.
