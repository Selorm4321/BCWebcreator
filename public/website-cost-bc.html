What actually drives the price

Six things move the number, and they are the same six questions asked on the scope call. None of them has a published figure attached, because each one changes with the business.

1. How many pages

A one-page site for a new business is a different job from a full site with a separate page for every service or every city you serve. Page count is the first thing that moves the number.

2. E-commerce, booking or members

Selling products, taking appointments, or gating content behind a login each add real engineering: payment flows, availability logic, and the testing that goes with them.

3. Whether copy is written or supplied

If you have your text ready, that is a straight build. If the words have to be written first, that work happens before anything can be laid out.

4. Photography

Existing photos cost nothing extra. Sourcing or shooting them is a separate piece of work, and it changes the schedule more than it changes the build.

5. Integrations

Payments, calendars, CRM, email marketing. Each connection is small on its own and adds up when several are needed together.

6. What happens after launch

Some owners want to edit their own pages and be left alone. Others want a monthly hand on updates. That choice is priced separately and never bundled in silently.

What a fixed price includes

  • Design and build, including a mobile layout tested on a real phone
  • Basic SEO setup: page titles, meta descriptions, structured data and a sitemap
  • Connecting your domain and hosting
  • Full ownership of the finished website, held by you rather than by BC Web Creator

Copywriting, photography, paid advertising and ongoing maintenance are not assumed: they are scoped and quoted as separate items when you need them, and nothing is added at the end without your yes. The written quote states what is in scope and what is not, so the boundary of the price is agreed before the build starts.

How the quote works

  1. Scope call. You describe the business and what the site has to do. A short call, not a sales pitch.
  2. One fixed price, in writing. Page count, features, inclusions and the launch window, in Canadian dollars.
  3. Work starts after you agree. The price does not change partway through, and nothing is added without your approval.

If something outside that scope turns out to be needed, it is quoted as a separate item before it is built, never discovered at the end.

How long it takes

Turnaround is agreed together with the fixed price, before work starts, and written into the quote. Sites with content ready land sooner than projects needing e-commerce, booking systems or large amounts of new copy. The exact window is set per project rather than published as a blanket figure, because a single number would be a guess for most jobs.
