How Much Does a Website Cost in Vancouver? (2026 Guide)

Prices range from $200 to $50,000 — so what's actually fair for a small business in BC? Here's an honest breakdown with no fluff.

By BC Web Creator · March 19, 2026 · 7 min read

If you're a small business owner in Vancouver, Abbotsford, or anywhere in the Fraser Valley, you've probably asked yourself: How much should I actually be paying for a new website in 2026?

The truth is, website pricing in British Columbia is all over the map. You can find someone on Fiverr who promises a site for $200, or a downtown Vancouver agency that won't return your call for less than $15,000. So what is a fair price — and what do you actually get for your money?

In this guide, we break down the real costs of web design in BC, what factors drive the price up or down, and how to avoid getting ripped off.

Quick Answer

For most small businesses in Vancouver or the Fraser Valley, a professional custom website costs between $1,200 and $5,000. Anything below $800 is likely a template with minimal customization. Anything above $8,000 is typically for large e-commerce stores or complex custom platforms.

The 3 Tiers of Web Design Pricing in BC

When shopping for a website, you generally have three options. Here is what you can realistically expect from each tier.

Tier Price Range Best For
DIY (Wix, Squarespace) $0 – $600/yr Hobbyists, very early-stage startups
Local Freelancer / Small Studio $1,200 – $5,000 Small businesses, service providers, restaurants
Mid-Size Agency $5,000 – $15,000 Growing companies, multi-location businesses
Large Vancouver Agency $15,000 – $50,000+ Enterprise, complex e-commerce, custom platforms

Tier 1: The DIY Approach ($0 – $600/year)

If you build it yourself using Wix, Squarespace, or Shopify, your upfront cost is mostly just your time. You'll pay a monthly subscription fee (usually $25 to $50/month) plus the cost of your domain name (~$20/year).

The catch is that DIY websites are cheap for a reason. They use generic templates that hundreds of other businesses are also using, they load slowly, and they almost never rank well on Google because the technical SEO is too basic. If you are relying on Google to find new customers, a DIY website will likely disappoint you.

Tier 2: The Local Freelancer or Small Studio ($1,200 – $5,000)

This is the sweet spot for the vast majority of small businesses in the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver. You hire a local expert to build a custom, professional site tailored to your brand and your city.

At this price point you get a unique design, mobile optimization, foundational local SEO (so you actually show up when people search for your service in your city), and a real person to call when something needs updating. This is exactly what BC Web Creator offers — starting at $1,200.

Tier 3: The Downtown Vancouver Agency ($10,000 – $50,000+)

If you hire a large marketing agency in downtown Vancouver, you are paying for their office rent, account managers, project coordinators, and large teams. The quality can be excellent — but so is the price tag.

This tier makes sense for large e-commerce stores with thousands of products, complex custom software, or enterprise companies with significant marketing budgets. For a local landscaper, salon, or contractor in Abbotsford, it is complete overkill.

What Actually Determines the Cost of a Website?

Why does one website cost $1,500 and another cost $8,000? It almost always comes down to these four factors:

📄

Number of Pages

A one-page landing site is significantly cheaper than a 15-page corporate website. Each page requires design, copywriting, and SEO work.

🛒

E-Commerce Functionality

Selling products online requires payment gateways, product databases, inventory management, and security. This always adds significant cost.

🎨

Custom Design vs. Templates

Buying a $50 WordPress template and changing the colors is cheap. Designing a site from scratch to match your brand perfectly takes time and real skill.

🔍

SEO Integration

A website is useless if no one can find it. Good SEO built into the foundation — optimized load speeds, meta tags, schema markup — takes expertise and time.

The Hidden Costs: What Else Do You Need to Budget For?

When budgeting for your website, don't forget these ongoing costs that exist regardless of who builds your site:

Item Typical Cost Notes
Domain Name ~$20/year e.g., yourbusiness.com — register through GoDaddy, Namecheap, or Google Domains
Web Hosting $10 – $50/month Where your website files live. Firebase, Netlify, and SiteGround are popular options.
SSL Certificate Usually free Most modern hosts include this. Required for the "https://" padlock in browsers.
Monthly Maintenance $50 – $150/month Optional but recommended. Covers updates, backups, security, and small content changes.
Professional Copywriting $300 – $1,000+ If you don't want to write your own page content. Often worth it for SEO.

5 Red Flags When Shopping for a Web Designer in BC

Not all web designers are equal. Watch out for these warning signs before you sign a contract:

  1. 1
    No portfolio or case studies. Any legitimate web designer should have examples of past work they can show you.
  2. 2
    They own your domain or hosting. You should always own your own domain name. If they register it in their name, you could lose your website if you ever switch providers.
  3. 3
    No mention of SEO. A beautiful website that nobody can find on Google is a waste of money. SEO should be part of every web design conversation.
  4. 4
    Vague pricing with no scope. If a designer can't tell you what's included for the price, expect scope creep and surprise invoices later.
  5. 5
    Prices that seem too good to be true. A $300 website is almost always a template with your logo slapped on it. It will look like every other $300 website.
